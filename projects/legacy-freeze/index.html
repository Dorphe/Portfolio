Legacy

Freeze Your Sperm

Discovering the reasons behind Legacy's sperm freezing page's low performance and why education is the critical missing ingredient when selling a high life-impact product.

The short version

Challenges

  • Designing a page that can be used as both a landing page and further information, covering the entire conversion funnel
  • Going against best practices in the service of your own design problems

Key learnings

  • Purchasing a healthcare product isn't your standard online transaction
  • Longer purchasing cycles fundamentally change how you market a product
  • Testing with real prospective customers can get you very valuable data, cheaply and quickly
  • Using simple, first-degree language is necessary in the context of an unaware customer

Results

Metric Result
Revenue ↑ 126%
Average Order Value ↑ 54%
Conversion rate vs. Shop All page 140%

Uncovering the problem

The freezing page was the only place on the website dedicated to explaining sperm freezing. That meant it was used in a lot of contexts, mostly as a landing page when coming from a social media ad campaign.

Quantitative data showed it had a high bounce rate and low conversion rate. A high bounce rate often indicates the content didn't appeal or respond to expectations, and a low conversion rate tends to follow that up. That was the red flag we needed to investigate.

The data

Legacy has a culture of regularly capturing user sentiment through user interviews and surveys. This available information delivered a great launch pad for investigation. Here are some questions we already knew users were asking:

  • "First I need to know more about the process, I want to understand how it works and be sure that this is safe."
  • "How can I be sure you'll be able to get my sperm and freeze it safely?"
  • "I need to feel like you are understanding my situation in order to provide the right solution."

Creating awareness

The question "Why should I freeze my sperm?" is one most didn't have an answer to — in fact, the level of education around sperm is astonishingly low. Sperm freezing being a fairly niche procedure, the marketing strategy was focused on creating awareness: "Why should I freeze my sperm?"

Creating awareness is the first step in the marketing funnel — after all, who's going to buy a product without knowing it even exists? This also means what comes next needs to answer the question: "Why should I freeze my sperm and how do I do it?"

Understanding the problem

Let's do a quick recap. We had a page with a high bounce rate and low conversion rate, a target audience predominantly at the unaware stage, and a marketing strategy aimed at creating awareness. A high bounce rate indicates the content fails to meet users' expectations.

1. Value proposition

"Avoid the clinic" answers the "how", but not the "why" you should freeze your sperm. Knowing you can do it from home is interesting, but not the main driver at this stage of awareness.

2. Pricing shown too early

Price isn't the main concern at this stage — the customer still doesn't know what sperm freezing is or why they should do it. From previous pricing experiments, we discovered a high amount of price elasticity. We saw little correlation between price and number of orders, meaning customers are willing to pay if they believe the solution is right for them.

3. Wrong imagery

Showing the physical product as the hero doesn't communicate what Legacy is fundamentally selling — preserving your ability to have children. The product isn't the seller, the outcome is.

The education gap

The page rarely took the opportunity to educate. Most of the questions that were identified came late (if not at the very end) in the experience, way past the point where we see users drop off.

  • More than half of men don't know what affects their fertility
  • 70% of people overestimate the age at which male fertility decline begins
  • 72% of people believe that taking testosterone improves a person's fertility — it actually brings it down to 0

The hypothesis

By focusing on education and human stories, AOV & conversion will go up. Sell the dream of having children, not a green box.

The solution

Telling a story Having a family is fundamentally a human thing — the old page had an anemic amount of actual humans on it. Taking a more direct human approach to how we communicate should help customers understand what Legacy is proposing.
Use cases & familiar language Legacy has a history of using complicated or overly second-degree language. We clearly showed use cases by saying "you should freeze your sperm if" so users could recognize themselves in those situations. Outcome: users can understand, relate, and insert themselves in situations.
The product grid By breaking down the bundles into their functions rather than their contents, we hit the double whammy of education and explanation. 88% of users already referred to product bundles informally by their timeline. 62% preferred first-degree language. 25% were actively turned off by flowery language. We realized Legacy was essentially selling insurance — "we're not selling t-shirts."
How it works By distilling the steps of ordering and using the kit into 5 simple steps, we eased prospective customers into the idea that it's a quick and easy process. Asking customers to trust us with their genetic material is a big ask, so we needed to answer their logistics and security questions in-depth.

Test results

Now that we've executed on our hypotheses, let's see how it performed.

The wins

  • 3/6 users mentioned they like the brand — what we're doing with the brand is inspiring trust
  • 6/6 mentioned the vast majority of the information was new to them
  • 6/6 mentioned nothing was confusing — "it's written in a very cohesive language, it's very easy to understand"
  • 5/6 contributors mentioned they had no unanswered questions about sperm freezing
  • 6/6 were drawn to the bundles comparison table section

Further improvements

  • We still haven't cracked convincing potential proactive freezers
  • Need to figure out whether stats and data about fertility decline resonates with users
  • Need to consider whether to mention testing on this page, balancing user needs versus business goals

Key learnings

  • Purchasing a healthcare product isn't your standard online transaction. Longer purchasing cycles fundamentally change how you market a product.
  • Using simple, first-degree language is necessary in the context of an unaware customer.
  • Best practices aren't always gospel. Sometimes you have to find your own design solutions tailored to your problem.

Legacy

Freeze Your Sperm

Legacy's sperm freezing page had low conversion and high bounce rates. The fix was education: selling the outcome, not the product.

The short version

Challenges

  • Designing a single page that serves as both a landing page and a deep-dive, covering the entire conversion funnel
  • Knowing when to break from best practices to solve a specific problem

Key learnings

  • Healthcare purchases have longer cycles that change how you market
  • Testing with real prospective customers yields valuable data, fast
  • Simple, first-degree language is essential when customers are unaware of the category

Results

Metric Result
Revenue ↑ 126%
Average Order Value ↑ 54%
Conversion rate vs. Shop All page 140%

Uncovering the problem

The freezing page was the only place on the website that explained sperm freezing, and it was used in nearly every context: social media ads, organic search, email campaigns.

Analytics showed a high bounce rate and low conversion rate. Content wasn't meeting expectations, and visitors were leaving before engaging.

The data

Legacy regularly captures user sentiment through interviews and surveys. Existing data surfaced three recurring concerns:

  • "First I need to know more about the process, I want to understand how it works and be sure that this is safe."
  • "How can I be sure you'll be able to get my sperm and freeze it safely?"
  • "I need to feel like you are understanding my situation in order to provide the right solution."

Creating awareness

Most people have no answer to "Why should I freeze my sperm?" Education around sperm health is surprisingly low, and freezing is a niche procedure. The marketing strategy was focused on creating awareness.

Awareness is the first step. Once someone arrives on the page, it needs to answer two questions: why freeze, and how does it work.

Understanding the problem

High bounce rate, low conversion, an audience at the unaware stage, and a marketing strategy focused on awareness. The content wasn't meeting expectations.

1. Value proposition

"Avoid the clinic" answers the "how", but not the "why." Knowing you can do it from home is useful, but not the main driver at this stage of awareness.

2. Pricing shown too early

At this stage, the customer doesn't even know what sperm freezing is. Price isn't the main concern. Previous pricing experiments showed high price elasticity: customers are willing to pay if they believe the solution is right for them.

3. Wrong imagery

Showing the physical kit as the hero doesn't communicate what Legacy is actually selling: preserving your ability to have children.

The education gap

The page rarely educated. Most answers to user questions appeared late in the experience, well past the point where visitors were dropping off.

  • More than half of men don't know what affects their fertility
  • 70% of people overestimate the age at which male fertility decline begins
  • 72% of people believe that taking testosterone improves a person's fertility — it actually brings it down to 0

The hypothesis

By focusing on education and human stories, AOV & conversion will go up. Sell the dream of having children, not a green box.

The solution

Telling a story The old page showed almost no people. Having a family is deeply personal. A human-centered visual approach helps customers connect with what Legacy is actually offering.
Use cases & familiar language Legacy had a history of using complicated or overly clever language. We switched to direct phrasing like "you should freeze your sperm if..." so users could recognize themselves in the scenarios.
The product grid We broke down bundles by function instead of contents. 88% of users already referred to bundles by their timeline. 62% preferred first-degree language. 25% were actively turned off by flowery copy. Legacy was selling insurance, not t-shirts.
How it works We distilled the process into 5 steps to show it's quick and straightforward. Asking someone to trust you with their genetic material is a serious ask, so logistics and security questions needed clear, upfront answers.

Test results

We ran moderated tests with 6 prospective customers.

The wins

  • 3/6 users mentioned the brand inspires trust
  • 6/6 said the majority of the information was new to them
  • 6/6 found nothing confusing: "it's written in a very cohesive language, it's very easy to understand"
  • 5/6 had no remaining questions about sperm freezing
  • 6/6 were drawn to the bundles comparison table

Further improvements

  • We still haven't cracked convincing potential proactive freezers
  • Need to figure out whether stats and data about fertility decline resonates with users
  • Need to consider whether to mention testing on this page, balancing user needs versus business goals

Key learnings

  • Healthcare purchases have longer cycles that fundamentally change how you market a product.
  • Simple, first-degree language is essential when customers are still at the awareness stage.
  • Best practices aren't always applicable. Sometimes you need design solutions tailored to your specific problem.

Smooch / Zendesk

Web Messenger 5

Smooch's Web Messenger had accumulated tech and design debt. As the company expanded into the enterprise market, it needed a ground-up redesign to support rich conversational interactions at any scale.

About Smooch & Zendesk

Smooch built omnichannel messaging solutions for SMBs and ISVs. Zendesk acquired them in 2019, and the product became a core part of Zendesk's offering as Sunshine Conversations.

Responsibilities

  • Product design lead
  • Point resource for external collaborators
  • Design system manager

The objectives

1 — Flexibility at any scale Create a modular, customizable messaging client that works for small businesses and enterprise organizations alike.
2 — Rich conversational interactions Enable interactions that weren't possible through chat before: placing orders, shopping, canceling plane tickets.
3 — Shared design components Share messaging components between the consumer client and the agent dashboard, reducing translation layers for support agents.

Chapter 1 — Flexibility at any scale

Part of the strategy involved selling to ISVs, so the client had to be fully white-label with extensive theming capabilities.

I designed a customization framework with variables exposed through the API. Developers could reference these tokens to implement branded designs at scale.

A framework for every team

Small businesses could customize colors at a high level using simple options. Larger companies with development resources could engage at a much more granular level using variables. Expedia was one of the first to adopt the granular framework.

Chapter 2 — Conversation extensions

By 2018, consumers expected to handle business through text. Web Messenger 5 was designed to enable interactions that weren't previously possible through chat: placing orders, shopping, canceling plane tickets.

Use cases shipped

  • Buying designer shoes through conversation
  • Canceling a flight
  • Ordering food
  • Receiving order updates
  • In-conversation validation forms

The north star was to make Web Messenger a platform. Businesses could build their own integrations or plug in third-party services like Calendly or Shopify directly into the conversation.

Chapter 3 — Conversational design components

Mid-project, Zendesk acquired Smooch. Zendesk was already one of Smooch's biggest partners, and the two design teams had an existing relationship. The acquisition expanded the scope: Web Messenger would integrate into Zendesk's design system.

The teams aligned through remote collaboration and an intensive workshop week at Zendesk's Dublin office.

Shared components

The most ambitious goal was sharing the composer and conversation components between the messaging client and the agent dashboard. Since Smooch was an omnichannel API at its core, this reduced translation layers: agents would see exactly what customers saw.

Conclusion

Web Messenger 5 became the foundation for Zendesk's current AI-powered chat widget, used by thousands of businesses for customer engagement.

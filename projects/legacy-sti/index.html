Legacy

STI Test Results

STI testing is required by law in many US states before freezing sperm. The challenge: designing a result interface that is both accurate and emotionally appropriate.

The problem

In early 2022, Legacy expanded its offerings to include STI testing, both as a standalone product and as a legal prerequisite for sperm freezing in many US states.

STI results are binary. My first instinct was to use familiar semantic cues: green/red, checkmarks/crosses. That created an immediate problem.

Testing positive for an STI is, technically, positive… but it's not good news. In any other context, ✅ = 😀. However, in this case, it's very much ☹️.

The hypothesis

Associating positive semantic visuals with a negative emotion creates confusion.

The process

I tested iterations with green/red and icon systems. Reception was poor. Users were confused about whether a "positive" result was good or bad news.

I consulted with healthcare experts and studied how traditional lab results handle this. Clinical test results are deliberately plain: text only, no color-coding. The semantic weight of colors is culturally conditioned, not medically appropriate.

The result

The final design follows the format of a traditional lab result sheet. It may look less polished at first glance, but it's familiar to both clinicians and patients, and avoids the semantic confusion entirely.

Key design decisions

  • No color-coded status indicators. Results are communicated through plain text, not color
  • Clinical table format. Familiar to anyone who's received lab work from a doctor
  • Clear "your next steps" section. The result alone is not enough. Patients need guidance on what to do next
  • Contextual recommendations. Different guidance shown depending on whether a result is negative, positive, or inconclusive
